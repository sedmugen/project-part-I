Introduction
Purpose and intended use
This document establishes DailyKhata's formal requirements baseline for the project team, supervisor and assessors. It translates the approved problem, user needs and scope into eight business requirements, 69 functional requirements and 26 non-functional requirements. The catalogue supports architecture, database and interface design, implementation and acceptance testing. It specifies intended behaviour; it does not assert that the application has been built or tested. [G1-G5, D1, D2, A3]
All 38 canonical scope capabilities are represented, including all 27 Must Have capabilities. Optional requirements retain their approved Should Have or Could Have status. The baseline is feasible as a bounded four-student integration project using existing OCR and language-model capabilities, subject to the dependency and acceptance-target reviews recorded later in this document.
Project overview and problem
DailyKhata is a personal expense management application primarily designed for Pakistani users who frequently deal with paper receipts, cash transactions and informal expense tracking. It reduces the effort required to maintain personal or household expense records through receipt digitisation, OCR, quick cash expense capture, manual expense entry, automatic categorisation, user confirmation and correction, a structured Chart of Accounts, transaction history, spending summaries, item-level price history, semantic product classification and an AI Financial Assistant.
Paper receipts and unrecorded cash purchases leave personal and household expense histories incomplete. Manual upkeep makes consistent recording difficult. Users need low-effort capture and reliable ways to review and understand spending.
Product boundary
The primary users are homemakers and household budget managers, university students and salaried professionals in Pakistan. A single account holder records personal or household expenses. Small shopkeepers are secondary direct users for petty cash and supplier expenses. Family members may review information with the account holder or receive a user-exported summary; they do not gain household roles, invitations or collaborative editing rights. The system is mobile-first and targets ordinary Android-class devices. [D1, D2, G1]
The current product combines reviewed receipt digitisation, manual and text-based cash entry, an expense ledger, internal accounting mappings, semantic product classification, item-level price history, summaries and a read-only AI Financial Assistant. Voice, bilingual localisation, export, custom labels, extra views and image archive remain optional. Detailed exclusions and future scope appear later and are not current requirements.

Business requirement analysis
The first six outcomes define the minimum product purpose. BR-007 and BR-008 add optional benefits without changing the intended market. No requirement promises measured financial savings, user retention or a market share. A saving suggestion is useful only when its arithmetic and assumptions are visible; the user remains responsible for deciding what to do. An expense record can be internally coherent even when the user has not recorded all real-world spending, so the assistant must distinguish recorded spending from total actual spending.
Functional requirements
Shared workflow and data rules
A capture route produces a draft. Extraction and interpretation propose values. The account holder reviews required fields, resolves inconsistent totals and confirms categories, then explicitly commits the expense. Only an active committed expense participates in summaries and price history. Every protected operation inherits the authentication boundary. The common actor, preconditions, trigger and rationale stated for each subsystem apply to its records; each requirement supplies its own behaviour, acceptance oracle and failure path.

Authentication and access
Context: Identity establishes the account boundary used by every later workflow.
Actor and preconditions: Unauthenticated visitor for registration and login; account holder for logout. Credentials are supplied through the application; protected data has an owner.
Trigger and common flow: Register, sign in, sign out or request protected content. The specific requirement below defines the observable result; invalid operations follow its failure path and do not imply a successful save.
Account registration
FR-AUTH-001 | Must Have The system shall create a distinct user account from a valid, unused login identifier and accepted credentials.
Trace and rationale: BR-005; SC-01; DR-09; EV-07. This behaviour implements the subsystem purpose above and the linked business outcome.
Acceptance and expected result: Submitting an unused valid identifier creates one account; submitting the same identifier again creates none and returns a non-disclosing failure message.
Alternative or failure: Invalid or duplicate input leaves no partial account.
Dependencies and quality: No earlier functional service required; authentication infrastructure is needed. NFR-SEC-001, NFR-SEC-002, NFR-SEC-003, NFR-SEC-005, NFR-COMP-001.

Dependencies and quality: No earlier functional service required; authentication infrastructure is needed. NFR-SEC-001, NFR-SEC-002, NFR-SEC-003, NFR-SEC-005, NFR-COMP-001.

Constraints
The project is a four-student BS Computer Science FYP at Beaconhouse National University for academic year 2026-2027 under Nouman Ali. The approved scope, single-account boundary and existing delivery priorities constrain implementation. No exact development budget or confirmed submission deadline is established for D3. The team must select a financially feasible deployment during architecture work; this document does not invent a budget ceiling or a semester delivery schedule.
The interface is oriented to personal and household expenses, not journal-entry operators. The baseline currency is PKR and the initial interface can be English. Full English and Urdu localisation is Should Have. Privacy, source provenance, corrected categories and coherent monetary totals are constraints on every implementation choice. No vendor, programming language, mobile framework or database is mandated.

Out of scope

Rule
Normative interpretation and reason
E-01 Identity and dates
Stable opaque identifiers distinguish account, expense, item, category and account mapping. Use the expense calendar date for summaries and a separate creation timestamp. Resolve relative dates using Asia/Karachi. Today may be a visible default in manual entry; missing receipt dates require confirmation. This formalises retrospective entry.
E-02 Amounts and currency
Baseline expenses are positive PKR amounts represented exactly to two decimal places. A manually entered total is permitted when OCR fails. Refunds, income workflows, foreign-currency conversion and scheduled future expenses are not added by this specification.
E-03 Receipt reconciliation
The payable transaction total is authoritative after review. Allocate it across the categorised purchase lines, including explicit user-reviewed discount, tax or rounding adjustments. Keep adjustments distinct from products and exclude them from product-price history. Block saving until the allocations equal the total; do not invent a missing product to balance it.
E-04 Counting and categories
For an itemised receipt, category totals sum its allocations; for an unitemised expense, its single allocation equals the total. Never sum both parent and line totals. The transaction primary category supports display and search; a category filter also matches a line allocation. It does not replace mixed-item allocations.
E-05 Product quantities
Keep purchased count, pack size, unit price and line total distinct. Unknown quantity is not silently one. Convert known compatible units, such as g to kg, and show the comparison basis. Separate brands and pack identities even under the same canonical product type.
E-06 Corrections and deletion
Preserve original OCR text alongside corrected values while a record exists. Deleting the expense removes its financial content under NFR-PRIV-003; source preservation does not mean retaining deleted content forever. Ordinary history excludes deleted records. Corrections invalidate dependent calculated results.
E-07 Account framework
Maintain the five conventional account types: asset, liability, equity, income and expense. The FYP exposes only expense-management workflows; cash is an asset account. Recognising an account type does not create sales, payroll or full financial-statement functionality. Final hierarchy and codes remain architecture decisions.
E-08 Settings and credentials
A display name and default summary month give a bounded interpretation of basic profile and settings. Login identifier format and credential rules are documented at implementation review; if passwords are managed locally, use at least 12 characters and permit at least 64. These proposed details do not add social login or account administration.
E-09 Draft lifetime
Core draft recovery is limited to the current open authenticated session. Surviving application restart without connectivity is the Could Have offline-draft feature. Pending drafts never count as expenses. Optional archive retention does not make permanent image storage core.
E-10 Optional data output
CSV and/or PDF means at least one format, not both. If CSV is selected, user-supplied text beginning with formula-control characters must be exported as inert text and fields escaped correctly. Household output remains an account-holder-controlled selection, not a shared-account service.
Fixture
Definition and measurement control
T1 Ordinary device and network
Android 11-class device, 4 GB RAM, at least a 360 CSS-pixel-equivalent viewport; one newer Android version. Choose and record native or mobile-web delivery. Test network: at least 10 Mbps down, 2 Mbps up and approximately 100 ms round-trip latency. Record actual conditions, device, build, cold/warm state and provider configuration.
T2 Capacity and ledger
Ten isolated test accounts, each with 1000 active expenses and up to ten line items per receipt. Run 20 minutes at one request per account every five seconds, 20 percent writes and the remainder history, search and summary reads. Verify all committed records and report rejected requests separately.
T3 Security and privacy
Two accounts with deliberately overlapping merchant and product names but distinct private records; expired sessions, altered object identifiers, malicious filenames, invalid images and cross-account assistant prompts. Include receipt text attempting to override retrieval instructions.
T4 Financial oracle
Controlled PKR records with known dates, categories, product identities and expected arithmetic. Include empty data, two periods, zero baseline, repeated expenses, mixed receipt, missing unit, corrected item and deleted transaction. Reference acceptance scenarios in the validation section.
T5 Receipt extraction
Twenty consented or synthetic printed receipts, clearly labelled by origin, with 3-12 purchase lines each, legible English or Latin-script text, varied layouts and known payable totals. Use two independent annotation passes and reconcile differences. Keep several blurred/cropped images separate for failure-path tests. Urdu OCR is not implied by Urdu interface support.
T6 Prospective user and language review
Five recruited trial users spanning the three primary groups for core tasks; a competent bilingual reviewer for optional Urdu output. Record consent, task script, actual outcomes and defects. These participants have not yet been recruited or tested.

Term
Meaning in this specification
DailyKhata
The personal expense-management application defined by the approved scope.
OCR
Optical character recognition; proposes text from receipt images.
Receipt digitisation
Capture and transformation of a receipt into reviewable structured expense data.
Expense transaction
One confirmed expense with an owner, date, amount and categorised accounting allocations.
Line item
An individual purchase line within a receipt; distinct from the transaction total.
Automatic categorisation
A machine proposal for transaction or line-item category, subject to user confirmation or correction.
Expense category
User-understandable spending classification; its label is separate from its identity.
Chart of Accounts
Internal register of stable accounting accounts, types and references.
Semantic product classification
Mapping to canonical product types while preserving source text, brand and product identity.
Item-level price history
Historical recorded product prices with purchase context and comparable quantity where available.
AI Financial Assistant
Read-only conversational analysis of the current user's authorised DailyKhata records.
Localisation
Optional adaptation of interface language and presentation, including potential Urdu RTL support.
PKR and Rs.
Pakistani rupee; the baseline currency. Amounts use exact two-decimal representation.
RTL
Right-to-left text and interface presentation where required for Urdu.
FYP and SRS
Final Year Project and Software Requirements Specification.
Draft and commitment
An unconfirmed working record and the explicit transition to one saved expense. Drafts do not contribute to financial totals.
